# Make learning repeatable

Use this when the next step is clear and the user needs a practical way to start, keep going, or resume. Build the routine around one useful action that changes a project decision or improves a craft outcome.

## Make the first useful action easy

| Design choice | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anchor** | What already happens reliably just before this could start? | After the Monday planning note, choose the open assumption to test this week. |
| **Visible cue** | What should be open, laid out, or on the calendar beforehand? | Leave the latest interview question and the redacted note template beside the calendar entry. |
| **Small start** | What first move can you do on a busy day? | Open the last observation and write one follow-up question. |
| **Appeal** | What makes this action feel worthwhile now? | Let a peer choose which of two useful questions to investigate, then compare what you each find. |
| **Closure** | What immediate feedback shows that the step happened? | Save the observation and one sentence about the decision it affects. |
| **Restart** | What is the next available cue if you miss the slot? | Use the next existing planning slot. Reduce scope if two missed sessions reveal the same obstacle. |

Pick a behavior that fits the kind of builder the user wants to be. Turn “I want to be less impulsive” into an observable action, such as writing one reason to change course and checking it before buying or building. Favor a step the user can repeat in their real environment. Increase difficulty when the smaller routine is stable and the extra effort buys useful learning.

Prepare a fallback. If an interview is cancelled, review an existing note, prepare a targeted follow-up, or reschedule. Record the fallback as preparation. Keep completed fieldwork, practice, and setup distinct.

## Keep evidence moving into decisions

- Choose a cadence that fits the work. For an active project, a weekly outside observation, interview, or trial is one possible starting rhythm. Have someone who can change the work take part directly. Recruit continuously if doing so lowers the cost of the next conversation.
- Use a short protected session for fieldwork or making. Move minor updates out of that block. Reserve room for cancellations, hard tasks, and rest. If the routine relies on overtime, cut the scope or change the rhythm.
- End with three lines. Record what you did, what you observed, and what decision is now easier or still open. Track genuine contact, use, or output separately from time and completed sessions. Revisit metrics that reward busywork.
- At review, compare intent with the calendar. Check the specific moment tasks stall, such as reaching for another idea after a difficult call. Reduce friction on the useful action and add a deliberate pause before the detour. When tiny preparation repeatedly stops short of contact, make the next target a completed outside action.
- Leave the next question, owner, and review date in the [decision brief](../assets/decision-brief.md). If new evidence removes the reason for the routine, stop it, park it, or redirect it to the current constraint.

Example routine. After the first team planning slot each week, pick one decision, inspect the last evidence note, and schedule the next relevant contact or observation. Review at the next planning slot. The number of slots kept measures consistency. What eligible people actually did bears on demand.
