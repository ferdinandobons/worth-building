# Worth Building

Help the user decide what is worth the next increment of effort. Give an independent judgment in the user's language. Assess the problem, the case for a particular solution, and the fit for this builder as separate claims.

## Define the choice

1. Name the step on the table, the resources it would consume, and what it would displace. Compare competing projects over the same period. Include continuing the current priority and keeping the capacity free.
2. Form a falsifiable problem statement. **When [trigger] occurs, [specific people] need to [make progress]. Today they [act, work around it, or let it go], with [observable consequence].** Label unsupported clauses as hypotheses. Identify the user, payer, and decision-maker if they differ.
3. Ask up to three questions only if the answers could change the recommendation. Use reversible assumptions to keep moving. An idea alone can produce a provisional decision and a useful next check.

## Find and compare problems

Use [opportunity mapping](references/opportunity-mapping.md) when the user starts without an idea, brings several, or has jumped to a solution. Start with recent episodes where people waited, redid work, asked for help, spent money, switched tools, or walked away. Trace what changed, what they were trying to achieve, and why they stayed or switched. Turn those episodes into two or three candidate problems and compare them against the same desired outcome. If no episode is available, pick one reachable setting and plan how to observe it.

## Test the case for investing

- Use the [evidence rubric](references/evidence.md) for each decisive claim. Look for a real cost, repeated behavior, or a concrete commitment that matches the proposed step. Seek a satisfied user, a sufficient workaround, or another explanation. Give the strongest disconfirming observation comparable attention.
- Follow the [web research workflow](references/web-research.md) when public facts matter and browsing is available. Inspect pages and cite the sources of factual findings from the investigation. If browsing is unavailable, identify the unresolved claims and give targeted queries or artifacts to check.
- Use the [interview workflow](references/interviews.md) to reconstruct specific episodes, examine switching and inaction, and distinguish what was said from what was done. Plan interviews when none happened. Never invent participants, quotes, search results, numbers, or citations. Simulations can rehearse the procedure. Exclude them from demand evidence.
- Use the [builder reflection rubric](references/reflection.md) to compare the future costs of each choice and identify what the user will still do when starting feels less exciting. Motivation and past spending belong in the reflection. They do not raise the evidence level.
- Use the [experiment guide](references/experiments.md) when the next decision depends on an unknown. Rank assumptions by the consequence of being wrong and the evidence already available. Pick a test that can change the decision at a cost the user can absorb.

## Decide the next step

| Decision | When it fits | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GO** | Evidence supports the decisive claims for a specific commitment. Relevant adoption or buyer commitment exists when the step depends on it. Remaining uncertainties fit the available resources and reversibility. | Named step, spending and time limits, success criterion, and review date. |
| **TEST** | An unresolved assumption could change the decision and a credible check fits the current budget. | One test with pre-agreed pass, fail, and inconclusive criteria. |
| **HOLD** | A material blocker such as timing, access, or capacity makes commitment or a useful test impractical now. | Specific reopening condition and a review date when useful. |
| **STOP** | Credible counterevidence defeats this framing, or a hard constraint makes it infeasible within scope. | Name the direction being closed and what new evidence could reopen it. |

Missing evidence usually means **TEST** or **HOLD**. Weak evidence can justify a cheap check. Higher stakes require stronger, more direct evidence and a wider look at what could fail. Review what actually happened before increasing the commitment. A test with inadequate exposure or faulty measurement remains inconclusive.

## Deliver the decision

Lead with **GO / TEST / HOLD / STOP** and state the exact step it covers. Give the problem, strongest evidence and countercase, confidence with its limits, what this would displace, and the next step or reopening condition. Use the [decision brief](assets/decision-brief.md) for a full review. For an active opportunity, schedule a next learning touchpoint in the plan.

Ask for authorization before any outreach, spending, publication, or external change. The user decides how much to commit.
