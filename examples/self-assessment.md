# Worth Building evaluates itself

**TEST** a bounded usability and repeat-use pilot. The skill exists as a local package. Whether independent builders rely on it for consequential choices is an open hypothesis.

## Problem hypothesis

Independent builders who regularly start or consider side projects struggle to distinguish their own excitement from external demand. When a new idea competes for a free evening, a weekend, or part of their budget, an informal AI chat or an unsystematic search may leave them with no explicit rule for what evidence to collect before they commit. This could lead to avoidable time and money spent. One first-person account informed this assessment.

| Claim | What is available | Strength and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The problem occurs | One builder described repeatedly starting projects and feeling that several consumed more resources than they were worth. | **C1** for the target problem. A single self-report describes a pattern. It supplies no specific documented episode, magnitude, or frequency. |
| The proposed workflow can be attempted | This repository contains a portable skill, a decision rubric, and example cases. | Direct inspection establishes what is in the package. Adoption and usefulness remain **C0**. |
| Builders prefer it to what they do today | A plain AI chat, a short checklist, a peer conversation, or an existing discovery routine could already be enough. | **C0** for preference or comparative value. These are plausible alternatives to check in interviews. |
| Builders install, follow, and return to it | No independent installations, completed sessions, repeat use, or paid demand data were collected for this opportunity assessment. | **C0**. Any local installation performed by the author demonstrates setup only. |

**Countercase.** Builders may already know that interviews and real usage matter. If the bottleneck is finding participants or making room for research, an extra decision document could make the process slower without changing behavior. In interviews, ask what happened the last time a builder tried to get evidence, and where the work stopped.

**Builder fit.** The maintainer has described a personal reason to care. Their ongoing support budget, recruiting access, and competing priorities are still unknown. Before starting the pilot, name the project or activity these hours would displace. If no useful slot or recruitment route exists, change the immediate decision to **HOLD** and set a review condition.

## First test

- **Assumption.** Builders with a live side-project decision can use the skill with little help and will choose it again for a separate decision rather than falling back to their existing process.
- **Who and how.** Invite six independent builders who faced a meaningful project choice in the past month and currently have two real decisions or decision points. Recruit through two routes if feasible. Reconstruct a recent decision made using their usual process. Give each builder access to the skill for the first live decision and leave its use optional for the second. The cases differ, so treat comparisons with the previous decision as qualitative. Ask permission to review redacted notes and record when someone takes a concrete outside step.
- **Primary measure.** Of the six eligible participants with access to the package and two qualifying decision points, count those who record a decision-changing unknown and take a checkable next action using the skill, and those who later choose the skill unprompted for another qualifying decision. Record setup time and friction. Feedback can explain behavior. Count actual follow-through.
- **Pass.** At least four of six use it independently and take the action, and at least two choose it again within 14 days. Consider another small pilot focused on who benefited and why.
- **Fail.** After all six have adequate exposure and a genuine second opportunity, at most one takes the action and none returns. Stop expanding this version and ask what their preferred alternative did better.
- **Inconclusive.** Recruitment misses the segment, second decisions do not occur, setup blocks exposure, or results fall between the thresholds. Diagnose that constraint before running another round.
- **Limit and review.** Proposed owner is the maintainer, with a cap of eight hours and no paid promotion over 14 days after recruitment starts. Review at the end of the window. The thresholds guide a small local decision. They do not establish prevalence, retention rates, or causality.

**Confidence.** Low that the proposed workflow changes how independent builders allocate time or money. The first budget goes to finding out.
